# User guide

## What the application does

Monido's mark is an M whose middle is a nest and whose right leg rises: family savings and their growth. The dashboard stores monthly balances, shows how net worth changes and helps you plan goals. It does not receive data from a bank: you enter it yourself. To work with an already installed site, all you need is a browser and the address from the owner.

## Signing in

1. Open the dashboard address.
2. Enter the family password given by the owner; the eye at the end of the field shows what you typed. Forgot it? Ask the family member who manages the settings; the server's owner can reset it ([Resetting the family password](deploy-docker.md#resetting-the-family-password)). The sign-in screen says so under the field.
3. After 10 wrong attempts within a minute, login from that address is blocked for a minute. If very many wrong attempts arrive from many addresses at once, login is paused for everyone for a minute.

On a new installation the owner opens the address within 15 minutes and sets the family password (12 to 128 characters, repeated twice). Telegram can be enabled afterward in Settings → Family and access. The project setup wizard opens next (see "Setting up for the first time"). If the time has passed, the screen says so and the owner restarts the app.

If the owner turned on sign-in through Telegram, there is no password: press "Sign in with Telegram", open the bot, press Start and then Confirm. You must have an account first (see "Family and rights"). The attempt lives 5 minutes. Confirm only a sign-in you started yourself: if someone sends you a sign-in link, ignore it.

Who is signed in is shown at the foot of the side menu, above "Settings": the button with the name opens what this person may do and "Sign out". The demo has no account button.

Everyone signed in sees the whole family database. Whether you can change it depends on your rights (see "Family and rights"). Do not give access to people who only need screenshots of a report.

In the [public demo](https://demo.monido.dev) the sign-in screen shows the demo password. Each visitor gets a private copy of a fictional family for 15 minutes: only you see your changes. The pill beside the page title counts the time down (on a narrow screen, tap it for the details) and turns orange in the last 2 minutes. When the time is up your changes are deleted; "Start over" opens a fresh copy without the password (at most once in 10 seconds). Each copy has room for about 16 MB of data: if a save says the demo database is full, start over. Signing out keeps your copy until its time is up: signing in again returns to it. The demo does not download a database copy. If the demo is busy, the sign-in screen says how many minutes to wait; if the demo server was restarted meanwhile, "Start over" brings you back to the sign-in screen with a note to sign in again.

## Install on your phone or computer

Monido can sit on the home screen like an app, without an app store: on an iPhone or iPad, open it in Safari, tap Share and "Add to Home Screen"; on Android, Chrome's menu → "Add to Home screen" or "Install app"; on a computer, the install icon in the address bar of Chrome or Edge. It then opens in its own window without the browser's address bar. It is the same site: it needs the connection to your server, and nothing is stored on the device for offline use. On an iPhone the installed app keeps its own sign-in, so you sign in once more there.

## Reading and typing comfortably

- Amounts can be typed with a comma or a dot (`1234,5` or `1234.5`), with at most two digits after it; the field groups the thousands as you type (`1 250 000`), and a pasted or dropped amount from a bank statement (`1.250.000,50 ₽`) is read with its grouping; extra decimals in a pasted amount are rounded (`0,125` becomes `0,13`). Backspace right after a thousands space removes the digit before it (Delete right before one, the digit after it). From 10 000 the amount is repeated in short under the field (`12.5M ₽`), except in privacy mode. Where an amount may be negative (an account balance), a pasted amount may start with any dash (`–500` reads as minus 500), and a phone shows a ± button in the field that changes the sign. A field that cannot be read turns red, and once you leave it a line under it says what is wrong.
- Dates are typed as digits only, in the order of the interface language (`15032014` becomes `15.03.2014` in Russian, `03152014` becomes `03/15/2014` in English); a one-digit day or month is padded when you type the separator (`5.3.14`); a two-digit year is read at once (`05.03.14` is 5 March 2014, so Enter right away saves that date) and is written out in four digits when you leave the field. A year outside 1900–2100 is taken for a typo. Backspace on a separator removes the digit before it. Under the field the date is repeated in words with its weekday, or a line says that such a date does not exist (30 February). A half-typed date is never saved and never erases the date stored before: Enter on it, or leaving the field, shows a line saying how to finish it, and Save in that form refuses with a note until the date is complete. The calendar button in the field opens the browser's date picker, where the browser has one. A goal's date is a month (`06.2031`): under it the field says how far it is ("In 4 years 8 mo") or that it has passed, and the buttons "In 1 year", "In 5 years", "In 10 years" fill it in one tap; a new month is saved as its last day.
- A short choice (a member, a currency, an account or loan type, years or months of a loan's term, the early-payment strategy, the property owner, the family language and currency) is a row of buttons: tap one to choose it, or move between them with the arrow keys. A long list stays a dropdown.
- Fields use 16px text, so a phone does not zoom in when you tap one.
- The theme is chosen per device. At the bottom of the side menu, next to the version, choose one of three icons: the monitor follows the device, the sun is light, the moon is dark. Hover over an icon to see its name. On a phone, open More first. The compact desktop menu shows the same icons in a column. These controls also work in the demo. System, the default, follows the device's setting and switches live; Light and Dark stay as chosen on this device. Both themes are tinted with the brand violet: the dark one is a deep violet-grey, not black. On the setup screen, the sun/moon button switches to the other theme.
- If the system asks for more contrast (macOS "Increase contrast", Windows contrast settings), text, lines and field edges get darker. In Windows high-contrast mode the selected menu item, the active switch and the current row keep a visible highlight.
- Nothing is pinned to the top of the page: a field reached with Tab is never hidden.

On a small screen, account heatmap month headings use1–12; each month's full name remains in its title. Wide loan and property tables scroll sideways inside their card, including their action menus. Confirmation windows scroll internally when the screen is short. Long chart values and goal statuses wrap or move to another row to stay readable; changing language or display currency does not change the underlying records.

## Entering data

Every field helps you type: see "Reading and typing comfortably" for amounts, dates, months and short choices, "Monthly update" for the check against last month's balance, "Loans" for the term and the payment, and "First setup" for the currency search and manual rates. Three more things hold everywhere:

- **Enter and Escape.** In a window or an inline form (an account, a month of an account, the edit of a loan or a property, a row of a table such as Goals) Enter saves and Escape cancels; the buttons inside a form never trigger on Enter, nor does Enter on a row's checkbox. A save already under way is not sent a second time.
- **Account names.** When you add an account, the name field suggests the names of accounts already there, so a repeated name is picked rather than retyped.
- **Time zone.** In the Telegram reminders block, "Detect" fills in the time zone of this device; under a valid zone the field shows its offset and the current time there ("UTC+5 · now 14:05"), kept current while the page is open, so you can check that it is the right one.

## Language

The interface is available in English, Russian, German, Spanish, French, Brazilian Portuguese and Simplified Chinese. The German, Spanish, French, Portuguese and Chinese texts were machine-translated and are being reviewed by native speakers; corrections are welcome (see `docs/translating.md`).

The interface language is the family's: it is set once in "Settings → General" and every device opens in it. Until the app has asked the server (the sign-in screen), it uses the language of your browser if it is supported, and English otherwise. The change applies at once, without reloading the page (the screen is redrawn, so save any edit you have not saved yet before switching).

The public demo is the exception: its sign-in screen has a language menu (each language by its own name), and the sandbox you open is in the language you chose; "Start over" keeps it.

The language is the same for everyone in the family. It changes only the interface text, numbers and dates; the data you entered (names of accounts and members) stays as you typed it. The language of Telegram reminders is the family language from "Settings → General", see "Telegram reminders".

## The menu

On a phone a bar at the bottom holds Overview, Close month, Assets and Income (the short name of Income and expenses), and "More" opens the menu as a sheet: the same tiles three across, with Settings as the ninth tile, and the account, the version and the theme at its foot. The side menu is a grid of tiles, two across: Overview, Close month, Assets, Loans, Income and expenses, Goals, Financial health and Advisor (the public demo has no Advisor). Under the tiles comes the second floor described below: the area name with its pages or, on Overview, "To do". At the foot of the menu Settings is a row, next to the account button, the version and the theme. Related records live under their financial area: Accounts, Real estate and Available money under Assets; categories under Income and expenses; goal editing under Goals. Settings holds General, Family and access, Notifications, Change history and Data. Under the tiles the menu has a second floor: on Overview it is "To do": one row per month to close, then what the modules ask for (goals short of savings, a health indicator in the red, an Advisor proposal waiting for your vote; nothing when there is nothing to do). On any other page it is the area name with the pages of that area, the current one marked. Below 1200px, where the menu is a drawer (and on a phone, where it is the More sheet), a selector below the page title opens the same area pages.

On a wide screen (from 1200px) the "Compact menu" button at the bottom of the menu shrinks it to icons, and the pages get that much more room; point at an icon to see its name. The choice is remembered in this browser. The gain shows on screens up to about 1700px wide: the page content itself is never wider than 1440px.

Each family member's name appears in their own pale colour in the "Accounts and properties" list and in "By member", so a person is recognisable across the pages; the colour follows the order the members were added.

While finished months wait to be closed, a number beside "Close month" says how many; it disappears once they are all closed.

Secondary sections fold into rows with a chevron on the right; click anywhere on a row to open it. Where it can, a closed row already says something the rest of the screen does not, such as the months of history or the part of a change that income and expenses do not explain. With the eye closed, amounts in those lines are hidden too.

Income and expenses has its own period (Month, Quarter, Year, All time), described below. “History” at the end of Assets has independent 3m, 6m, 12m and All choices, starting at 12m. Each shows that many months of change, so it starts at the balance that many months before the latest (12m runs from September to September), retains missing months as gaps, and sets the growth calculation range. Toggle net worth, assets and loans, and choose amounts or percentage change from the first month of the range. A missing or nonpositive base is explained instead of replaced by a later month; use “Show amounts” when percentages are unavailable. Point at or tap a month, or focus the graph and use the arrow keys, to read its values. The Overview's net-worth line always shows the thirteen months up to the chosen month; Goals and Financial health have no period.

**Currency conversion:** the dashboard opens in the family currency (set in "Settings"). The currency switch in the header is a quick view: it lists the currencies of the project ("Settings → General"), the family one marked "· family", up to four as buttons, more in a menu. Choosing another currency shows all totals in it, each amount converted at the rate on its own date (balances on the last day of the month, flows on the first day, goals today), so the history shows the real changes in that currency. The quick view lasts for this browser tab only: a new tab or visit opens in the family currency again, and choosing the family currency returns to it. The line under the page title shows the rate of the shown currency to the dollar ("1 USD = 83.99 RUB"); if the currency of a quick view is later removed from the project, the dashboard returns to the family currency. Rates of every project currency are downloaded from Frankfurter (central-bank rates). Amounts in a currency with no rate at all are left out of the totals, and the dashboard warns about them. A month whose income or expenses include such an amount has no totals: its savings rate, emergency fund and the health indicators built on them show "no data" rather than a figure built on a zero, and the averages leave that month out. Such a month is a gap on the income and expenses charts and its figures read `—`, and a total over several months that include it (the twelve months on Overview, a quarter or a year on Income and expenses) is `—` too. A loan or an account without a rate likewise leaves the health indicators built on it (debt to assets, the average debt rate, consumer debt, the payments' share, the emergency fund) without a figure, and a month whose net worth misses a balance or a rate shows `—` on Overview, with no change over the month or the year. The same month gives no net worth in its "How it is calculated" panel, no capital growth per year or real growth when it is the first or last month compared, and `—` in the Telegram /summary. Each health indicator waits only for what it reads: the emergency fund for the bank accounts, the consumer debt for the loans other than the mortgage, the payments' share for the loans with a payment this month. Claude, as the advisor, says plainly when the closed month's totals or the liquid money are unknown and does not grade a purchase or a credit against them. On the first open after an update the rates are loaded gradually: if you see a warning about a rate for another date, refresh the page once more.

**The version and updates.** The bottom of the side menu shows the version of the running application. When a newer release exists, the warning icon beside the page title shows "Version X is available" with a link to the package page and the command to update; the server's owner runs it ([Updating](deploy-docker.md#updating); the daily check sends no family data and can be switched off, see [Configuration](configuration.md#environment-variables)).

## What the application keeps

Every part of the application is always there: accounts and the "Close month" wizard, net worth, Income and expenses, Loans, Real estate, Goals, Financial health, the ✨ questions for Claude, the Advisor and Telegram reminders. A page with nothing entered yet shows what to enter and where, so the menu does not change as the data arrives. The Advisor and the reminders do nothing until they are set up (Telegram sign-in and an `https://` address for the Advisor's connector, a linked chat for the reminders); the public demo has neither.

**Track income and expenses.** One family setting in "Settings → General" (on by default; a manager changes it). Off, months are closed with balances only: "Close month" asks for no income, expenses or loan payments, and "Income and expenses" shows one notice in place of its page. The figures that need income and expenses (the savings rate, the emergency fund, the health indicators built on them, the Goals forecasts, statuses and shortfall) show "no data", not zeros, and the "short by" reminder does not appear. The month editor on "Accounts" then edits the balance only; the change shows in "Change history" as "Track income and expenses: Yes → No". Loading test data switches tracking back on. The income and expenses entered before stay in the database and come back when tracking is switched on again. An installation that had the old "Income and expenses" module off starts with tracking off.

An account link opens the matching account in "Assets → Accounts". If an old bookmark is ambiguous or the account no longer exists, no account is selected; choose one from the list.

## How to read "Overview"

On wide screens, the month-close action and currency selector sit beside the page title. Year cards stay separate from the capital chart. On phones, each owner's amount sits beside their name, with their share below; goals appear as rows with complete names. Asset totals and the savings rate are plain figures; colored badges identify statuses.

The page starts with the **years**: a cell per calendar year since the first closed month, the current year marked "· in progress", the net worth at the year's end in its head and the change over the year (over the closed months it has) under it. Inside each cell are twelve strokes, one a month: the height is the month's net worth over the whole history, the strokes are grey and the violet one is the month the page is showing, a dashed outline is the month not closed yet (a click opens "Close month"), an empty place is a month without balances. Click any closed month to move the page to it. With more than four years, "Earlier" and "Later" shift the window; on a phone one year shows at a time, picked with the pills above it.

Under the years comes the **net worth** of the chosen month: the amount with its change over the month and the year (the first recorded month says so instead), the line of the thirteen months up to it with the value marked (the axis in short amounts like $780K, a tinted band under the chosen month), under the line a column for each month's change, up for a gain and down for a loss from a zero line (the chosen month's in violet, the largest change signed; a month after a gap has none), and the assets bar: the violet part is owned outright, the grey part is still covered by loans, with both amounts and the assets total under it. When the history is shorter than thirteen months, the line shows what there is. The page opens on the last closed month; the choice is not kept between visits.

If a month is not closed, "Close …" is the button in the title row. What needs attention (savings short of what the goals need, health indicators in the red zone, Advisor proposals waiting for your vote) is listed under "To do" in the side menu. Then comes a row of key figures from "Income and expenses": the closed month's income, expenses and what is left (each with its change against the month before and, for what is left, the savings rate) and the emergency fund in months against the 6-month goal. Then the cards:
- **Whose assets**: a lane per owner with their share of the assets as a bar, the amount and their largest holdings;
- **Left after expenses · 12 months**: what the last twelve months left, the savings rate over them and a bar per month;
- **Goals**: a ring per goal (the first three) with what is saved of the target and by when, and a warning when the savings fall short of what the goals need;
- **Current stability**: a ring with an arc per health indicator (amber or red where it is) and how many are in a good zone at its centre, the worst zone under it, a chip per indicator coloured by its zone (green good or excellent, amber needs work, red critical) and, when something is below the norm, a line naming those indicators.

On a phone the figures go two across and the cards one under another. Below the summaries come unallocated income or expenses and data warnings. The full history of net worth with its growth per year ("History") is at the end of "Assets"; its legend button "In today's money" adds a dashed line of each month's net worth lifted to the last month's prices by the yearly inflation set in Settings → Parameters (one rate for the whole history, said under the chart; in "Change, %" it is the real growth). It is drawn when the dashboard is in the inflation's currency; in another one the button says so; the split of the change into saved cash and the rest ("What changed in net worth") is near the end of "Income and expenses". Detailed account lists and charts live in their own areas; contextual Claude actions remain on the detailed pages.

Assets, Financial health and Goals also show relevant missing-balance, missing income and expense and exchange-rate warnings beside their calculations. Open “Data warnings” for the affected records or rate dates. A rate fallback can affect a whole section, so it is not assigned to an individual row. An unavailable currency conversion is left out rather than shown as zero.

The line under the page title says which month the data is for (with income and expenses not tracked, the month of the balances). If it is two months behind or more, the line becomes a link to data entry and the age is highlighted. The icon with a triangle beside the title shows remarks about the data: missing balances and months. An account closed in the month-close wizard is not reported missing in the months after.

## Privacy mode

If a record or reference list cannot load, the page shows an error with Retry. Retry reads the failed data again and does not save a form. Previously loaded records and any open draft stay available; actions that need a list wait until it has loaded.

The eye beside the page title hides the amounts on the dashboard pages (Overview, Assets, Income and expenses, Goals, Financial health): every sum is drawn as `•••`, and the chart axes and tooltips show no amounts, only the shapes. Percentages, months and counts stay. The choice is kept in this browser only (it is not a family setting) and is off by default. In the Records section the eye hides the amounts you read (the lists, the loan summary and schedule, the early payments, the account history, the valuations, the goal list, last month's hints and the totals of "Close month"), but not the fields you type into; in Settings only the change history is masked, the rest holds no amounts. Press the eye again to show the amounts.

## The other pages

- **Assets**: one period for the whole page: Month, Quarter, Year or All time (against the previous month, three months back, twelve months back or the first month of the history; a period with no balances at its start cannot be picked, and the date compared with is written next to the switch). Every change on the page follows the period; the amounts and shares stay at the last closed month, what the family owns now. At the top: the total with its change and a sentence on which holding added and which lost the most (and whether that was the exchange rate alone); beside it the asset map, each tile's area its value (real estate, brokerage and bank accounts in their own colours, other holdings such as crypto in grey), named with its share, value, owner and change, a tile under 4% showing only its share; a tile opens its record. The map and legend show positive-valued holdings; zero and negative holdings remain in the full list and signed composition. "What made up the change" runs from the period's start total, a bar per holding (largest gain first, in the holding's map colour; "rate" when only the exchange rate moved), to the end total. "Composition" splits the assets by type, owner or currency (a split with one part is not offered), each part with its share and its change (a holding closed in the period counts in its part's change, so the parts add up to the total's change), and says how much is available at once. The history card shows the assets over the months around the period (6 for a month, 12 for a quarter, 2 years for a year, 36 months for all time), the period shaded with its change and the biggest month named; its link opens the detailed History. "Accounts and property" lists every holding by type with its owner, its amount in its own currency when that is not the dashboard's, its share and its change ("the EUR rate only", "no change", "closed in the period", or "no exchange rate" when a balance cannot be converted; "no rate at the start" when only the start cannot be converted; its change is unavailable); a name opens that very account or property on its records screen. Without an earlier month to compare with, the page shows the amounts alone. "History" at the end of the page gives the net worth over time with the growth per year and the doubling time (its axis shows short amounts like $150K, hidden in privacy mode). The debts are on "Loans": what the family owes today by the schedules, with the last closed month's figure under it; each loan in the list gives its balance today, rate, monthly payment, last payment and repaid share (the share of the original amount already paid off; none if the amount is unknown).
- **Income and expenses**: one period for the whole page: Month, Quarter, Year or All time, ending at the month shown between "‹" and "›" (the last closed month by default; "›" stops there, its tip says the next month is not closed yet; a month can also be picked by clicking its column on the chart). The address keeps the choice, so a reload or a shared link opens the same period. Under the title a sentence says what the period saved and its savings rate against the usual rate, and whether expenses were above or below a usual month (for a longer period, per month, with the category that grew most). **"Usual"** on this page is always the same thing: the median month of the six closed months before the period (fewer at the start of the history; all time has nothing before it, so no comparison); a quarter or a year is compared by its average month. The other pages keep their own baselines (the emergency fund and available money use the last three months). Then four figures (came in, went out, saved or "Overspent" in red, and the savings rate), each with its usual; the chart of income and expenses by month (two grey lines labelled at their right end, the gap hatched where the month saved and red where it overspent; the period shaded violet; the selected month's two points violet with their amounts; under it a strip of what each month saved, the period's bars violet, an overspent month red and named; a quarter or a month shows 13 months, a year 24, all time the whole history). "Where the money went": the kinds as one bar (mandatory, loans, by choice, not split), then the categories grouped mandatory / loans / by choice, largest first, each with its share, its amount, its usual (a category's usual is the median of the up to six months with categories before the period where it has an amount) and the change; "Not split" ends the list with the way to split it. Click a category to open it in place (on a phone it opens on its own screen with the way back): its months since categories began, its usual as a dashed line, one sentence with the largest month when it stands out, the accounts and other sources (a card, cash) it came from in the period's last month, and "Change the split of <month>"; click it again or press Escape to close it. Over a year or all time the card says how many months of the period have categories, and that there is no comparison when there are no categories before it. A category that had amounts before the period and none in it shows 0 with its decrease. When a line has no exchange rate its sum is unknown: it shows `—`, the bar of kinds and the shares are not drawn, and the card says why; the "Split" button is then not offered, and "Categories" says what is left cannot be counted. "Where it came from": active and passive income, the sources, what is not split, a one-off income (a bonus) when there is one, and who brought and who spent (loan payments count to the loan's owner; the Financial health cards for the FI rate and passive income lead here). "What changed in net worth": net worth at the end of the month before the period, what was saved, the rest (rates, revaluations, repaid loan principal, since expenses hold the whole payment, and corrections), net worth at the end; for the last closed month the records that moved the most beside it, each a link. "Will available money cover the coming months?" is a closed row at the end. While the last closed month has amounts without a category, the title row's button "Split <amount> <month>" opens "Categories" on that month.
  The "From income to savings" waterfall explains the last closed month: income, expense categories, any unallocated amount, and the remaining balance (which can be negative). Small categories are grouped as "Everything else", matching the allocation ring; "Unallocated" stays separate. When rates or category data are missing, or the category amounts do not reconcile with the expense total to the displayed cent, it shows aggregate expenses with an explanation. The chart scrolls within its card on narrow screens. "Amounts by step" exposes the full labels and values as text. Privacy mode hides the waterfall and that list entirely.

- **Financial health**: the stability summary counts reserve, debt, savings and growth indicators. Passive income and financial independence are shown separately and do not lower stability or create its priority warnings. Under "Current stability" the page shows how many of its indicators are in each zone, and a disclosure row per indicator (on every screen size; a row that needs work or is critical starts open), the worst zone first (critical, needs work, good, excellent): the value, the zone, the norm and advice, with a ✨ question for Claude. The reserve card names the month its figure is based on, beside the same figure on 3-month average expenses. A card links to the page that shows the indicator's trend and detail: savings rate, the share covered by passive income and income beyond salary on Income and expenses; debts and loan payments on "Loans"; real growth on its History. "Loan payments to income" is the share of the closed month's income that goes to the loans' scheduled payments for that month (the mortgage included; a payment shortened by an early payment counts as it is now): below 10% is excellent, below 30% good, 30–50% needs work, 50% and above is a high debt load. “Income beyond salary” measures passive income divided by total income; its detailed title is “Passive income share”. The emergency fund card also shows the cushion at the average expenses over 3 months. While income and expenses are not tracked, the indicators built on them show "no data".
- **Goals**: the title says what you save a month (the average of the last twelve closed months, so one repair or bonus barely moves it; the line under a goal also gives the last three months' average, to show a recent change) against what the goals need to make their deadlines, and how many make them. On the left is the list: the saving a month against the need as one bar, with "short by" in yellow when it falls short, then a row per goal in the order the savings go to them (the nearest deadline first): the share saved with a violet bar, "saved of target · by month" and whether it makes its deadline (green "on time for 05.2027", yellow "reached 05.2028, 5 mo late"; a goal never reached says why: nothing was saved in the last closed months, goals with nearer deadlines take all the savings, or not within 100 years at the current savings). Under the saving, "What if we saved a month" re-runs the plan for an amount you type: every goal's date and status, the shares and the independence forecast follow it, nothing is saved, and "Back to <your saving>" returns to the real plan (a goal on time does not get earlier with more: it already gets what its deadline needs). The page opens on the first goal that is late. The chosen goal on the right: "Update saved" (the page's one main button; it opens the editor on "Already saved") and the "···" menu (edit, delete); the figures (saved, needed a month, what it gets now and from when it gets more, and when it will be reached), its progress, and one sentence with the way out for a late goal ("On the plan it holds $54,230 by 12.2027, $8,770 short. To make it, save $2,816 a month, $675 more, or move the deadline to 05.2028"; the last words open the editor with that month). "How it accumulates" draws the plan from today's saved amount in ink, the dashed pace that would make the deadline, the target and the deadline; "How the goals share <saving> a month" shows two years of the saving split between the goals month by month (the chosen goal darkest) and a table in cents whose rows open a goal. For financial independence the figures are the forecast at 8% and the growth it needs, the chart shows that forecast and the range of returns from 5% to 11% (a band, with the month at 11% and at 5%), and "Forecasts" stays a closed row: capital growth per year over the whole history (your savings included; it links to "History" on Assets) and growth beyond savings (how fast net worth grew on its own after taking out what you saved), each with an ⓘ that shows how it is calculated with your numbers; then the capital that will grow on its own and the real return. Growth beyond savings needs at least 6 usable months and income and expenses tracked; loan payments are not counted as saving, so only the interest on loans lowers it; it also includes revaluations of real estate and exchange-rate moves, so it is not only market return. When one of the months used lost all the net worth it started with, the figure is not defined and the card says so. On a phone the list comes first and a goal opens on its own screen with "← Goals". With income and expenses not tracked, the page shows what the goals need, "no data" where the plan would say whether they make it, and a link to "Settings → General".

Red and green on these pages mean only an assessment and a signed change; expenses and debts are shown in plain text.

## Advisor: consult Claude

Each member can connect their own Claude account (claude.ai, Claude Desktop or Claude Code) to the family's numbers and ask whether the family can afford a purchase, a loan or a goal. You can first use the page’s manual consultation guide without connecting Claude. The connector needs sign-in through Telegram or the family password and an `https://` address. With Telegram each person connects Claude to their own profile; with the family password the connection is the family's: anyone signed in with the password can approve it, Claude enters data as the family (statements, loans, spending; proposals to the family need Telegram), and the Advisor page shows how many apps hold it and when it was last approved. With the family password, changing the password, switching the sign-in, loading the demo data or wiping the data disconnects Claude, and it is connected again from the same page; with Telegram sign-in, a switch of the sign-in disconnects everyone, while a change of the fallback password keeps the connections. When a condition is missing, the "Advisor" page says which (the server's owner fixes it, see [Claude's connector](deploy-docker.md#claudes-connector)).

**How to connect.** Open "Advisor → Ask Claude" and follow its steps:

1. In Claude (claude.ai), open Customize, then Connectors.
2. Choose Add custom connector.
3. Paste the address from "Advisor → Ask Claude" (click "Copy address") and give the connector any name.
4. Click Connect. If you are not signed in, confirm in Telegram (or enter the family password, when the family uses it). A screen of this app then asks "Allow Claude to act as <you>?" ("… as the family?" with the family password) and shows the app's name and the address the answer goes to (normally `claude.ai`). Check that you started this connection yourself, then click "Allow". Open that link in the same browser where you started: another browser gets an error and the connection has to be started again in Claude.

The page then lists the members who have connected, with you marked.

**What Claude can do.** Read the family's key numbers (net worth, savings, loans, goals), weigh a purchase, a loan or a goal with alternatives and a direct verdict, weigh repaying a loan early against investing the same money (with the emergency fund before and after), load a bank statement or a loan agreement into the month being closed (it shows a draft, writes it only after your "yes", and closes the month only when you say so; see "Loading statements and loans through Claude"; spending for months already closed is filed by category), and send one chosen scenario to the family for approval in Telegram (see "Proposals and approval" below). Every number comes from this app, not from Claude's own arithmetic; it asks for what is missing. Its alternatives may include a later or an earlier date for a goal; such a date can move a goal that is already late, and Claude says so (which goal, by how many months). Financial independence has one month there too, the plan's with its 8% return; a goal after it is funded from that month. Evaluations saved before 12 November 2026 keep the months they were made with. For a late goal it says what the nearer goals take and until when, and what the goal needs a month; it says when money starts going to a goal and how much a month then, and calls the goal's even monthly spread only that. Claude acts as the member who connected it, with that member's current rights: a member who may not edit data cannot save anything through Claude, and only a member with the vote right can send a proposal. It never moves money or sees bank accounts, and its edits appear in "Change history" as "<member> via Claude". Example questions are on the page, and the full English guide Claude follows is behind "Show the full guide". Claude's "now" figures are the averages of the last twelve closed months, the same as the Goals page plans with; when the last three months saved notably less, Claude says so.

**Without Telegram or HTTPS.** The person who runs the server can also connect Claude Desktop or Claude Code on that computer through the `monido-mcp` command, acting as a family member, with no sign-in and no public address; see [Claude Desktop or Claude Code on your own computer](deploy-docker.md#claude-desktop-or-claude-code-on-your-own-computer).

**Privacy and limits.** The answer is a calculation and a model's opinion, not a regulated financial consultation. Your conversation, and the numbers Claude asks for, go to Anthropic through your own Claude account; this app does not contact Anthropic itself. The public demo does not offer the connector.

**Disconnecting.** Click "Disconnect me" on "Advisor → Ask Claude" and confirm: Claude loses its access on your behalf, and you can connect again at any time. Deleting your user, or linking it to another Telegram account, also ends it. If Claude says it lost access (a token was used twice, so the whole connection was cancelled for safety), connect again: Customize, Connectors, then reconnect.

**After an update of the app.** claude.ai keeps the list of the app's tools from the moment you connected: a tool added later (for example, the preview of a proposal) does not appear in Claude until you reconnect. In Customize, Connectors, open the app's connector, choose Disconnect, then Connect, and allow it again.

### Loading statements and loans through Claude

With the Advisor on, Claude can fill the month you are about to close from the documents you send it. Statements work with the core alone (the balance); the income, money put in and spending fields need Income and expenses, spending by category needs Income and expenses, and loan payments need Loans and Income and expenses.

- **What to send.** A bank or card statement (a screenshot, PDF, Excel or CSV) for the month that is open in the wizard, one account at a time, or a loan agreement or a bank-app screenshot of a loan. Claude reads the document itself; the app never parses files. For a loan, Claude asks for the issue date, the original principal and the term if the document does not show them.
- **Draft, then "yes".** Claude shows what it understood in the chat: the balance, income, expenses, loan payments, category totals and any warnings, in words. Nothing is written until you say "yes" to that exact draft; a change means a new draft and a new "yes".
- **A new account or loan like an existing one.** If the name of an account Claude is about to add looks like one the same member already has in that currency, or a new loan looks like one of the family's loans ("Quillmark" and "Quillmark Gold", a typo, a different case or accent), Claude names the existing ones and asks whether it is new. It adds it only after you say so; otherwise it files the statement under the existing account.
- **Statements accumulate.** Each confirmed statement is saved as the draft of the open month (the same draft the month-close wizard shows). The account's figures and supplied category lines are saved together; if either cannot be saved, both stay as they were. Changes to a different account are kept. If this account or a loan line the statement names changed since the preview, Claude shows a fresh preview and asks for your confirmation again. Claude says which accounts are still missing. An account with no statement and no movement is carried over only when you say so. If the month-close wizard is open in a browser, reload it before typing or closing there: Claude's changes appear only after a reload, and stale browser writes are rejected.
- **Closing.** Close the month in the chat, by telling Claude to close it, or in the wizard as usual. If anything changed since Claude showed you the draft, it shows it again first.
- **Income and spending by category.** Claude files a statement's income and spending as the account's lines by category, the same lines "Break down" shows in the wizard, in one draft with the totals. The lines never add up to more than a total: what they leave is unallocated and shows as "Unallocated", and Claude says how much. When the statement prints no total, Claude takes the sum of the lines it read and says so. Income is what arrived on the account; transfers between your own accounts, refunds, loans received and asset sales are not income. For a brokerage account, income is dividends, coupons and interest, and expenses are fees and withheld tax; deposits, withdrawals and trades show only in the balance. Statements Claude filed before this version stay as "Other sources" named "Statement: <account>". If the same month also has an other source (a card, cash) in the same categories, Claude warns in the draft that the spending may be counted twice; check it before you say "yes".
- **A loan's remaining debt.** When a statement or a bank-app screen shows how much is left on a loan, Claude compares it with the loan's schedule in this app on the same day (the end of the statement's month, or the day the screen shows), in the loan's currency. If they differ by more than about one month's interest on the original amount (at least 1 unit of the currency, enough to absorb rounding and the bank counting interest by days), Claude shows you both numbers and asks whether an early payment is missing in the app or the bank recalculated the loan. It never changes a figure to make them agree and saves nothing about it: enter a missing early payment on "Loans". The bank's figure is not stored. If Claude was connected before this version, reconnect it to get this check (see "After an update of the app").
- **Not supported.** Reconciling a statement against what was entered earlier, keeping the original files, editing or repaying a loan early through Claude, and reopening a closed month. Transfers between your own accounts are left out; loan and card repayments count as loan payments.

### Proposals and approval

When a consultation ends with a plan, Claude offers to send it to the family for approval. You may change the title (up to 80 characters) or the short question (up to 300). First Claude shows you a preview: the card exactly as the family will get it in Telegram, in the family language, with the numbers worked out by the app; nothing is sent yet. Claude sends it only after your explicit yes to that preview; any change, or more than 30 minutes, means a new preview and a new yes. The title, the question and Claude's assessment carry no numbers: every number on the card comes from the app, and Claude tells you if they differ from what it said in the chat (the card rounds amounts to whole units). The proposal goes to everyone who has the vote right, a linked Telegram account and a way to vote: council proposals on (they get the card) or sign-in through Telegram on (they vote on the site). One author can have at most 5 proposals waiting for the family at once (open or sent back for revision); a sixth is refused until one is decided or withdrawn. Your own vote counts as "Agree" at once; if you are the only voter, the proposal is accepted straight away (the preview says so). A scenario Claude could not weigh for want of data cannot be sent: Claude says what is missing.

**The Telegram card.** Each voter gets one message from the bot: who proposes what, the question, the numbers "now → after" (the monthly payment of a loan, the share of income going to loan payments with the norm, the money left for living, the emergency fund in months (in months of the last twelve months' average expenses, and the loan share as of the closed month: the same figures as on "Financial health"), the goals that move; a number that stays the same is left out), Claude's assessment in a line or two, the votes so far ("Ryan ✓ · Annika ⏳ · decide by …") and the buttons "Agree", "Against" and "Details on the site". For a new goal or a saving, the card starts the numbers with the goal itself: by which month it is reached and what the app's plan pays into it a month ("Renovation: by December 2027, $1,429 a month"); when nearer goals take the savings first, it says from which month the money goes to it ("Renovation: by January 2029 instead of December 2027; the money goes to it from April 2028, $2,138 a month"); a goal out of reach says so. The bot writes in the family language ("Settings → General"): in Russian the two vote buttons read "I support" and "Against". The author gets a status message with "Withdraw". Every vote updates these messages in place; nobody gets a new message per vote.

**Voting with a comment.** After "Agree" or "Against" the bot asks for an optional comment: write one text message in the chat with the bot within 10 minutes, or press "No comment". Only a text message is a comment: a photo, a sticker, a voice message, a command such as /start or an edit of a message you already sent is not one. Until the comment, "No comment" or the end of the 10 minutes, the vote is not counted yet and can still be cancelled ("Cancel vote"). After an "Agree" you may still change it to "Against" while the proposal is open. When the 10 minutes pass, the vote counts without a comment. A text sent in the hour after that is not a comment either and gets one answer from the bot: the vote was recorded without a comment; while the proposal is open, it links to the proposal on the site (or names the Advisor page when the site address is not set), where you add the comment to your own counted "Agree" with "Add a comment" as long as the proposal is open; when the proposal is already back with its author for revision, replaced by a new version or decided, the bot says so instead. A comment on an "Against" cannot be added afterwards. Voting, the comment and "Withdraw" work whether or not "Bot commands" is on for you: that switch covers only /help and /summary.

**When it is accepted.** A proposal is accepted when every voter of its current version has agreed, the author included (their agree counts from the start). Everyone is then told "Decided ✓ …, agreed: …" with the comments; the author's own status message changes to the outcome instead of a second message (if they have since linked another Telegram account, or blocked the bot, they get the message too). The voters are fixed when a version is sent: a member who gets the vote right later votes from the next version on, and one whose vote right was taken away still votes on the version they were sent, in Telegram and on the site. Votes stop at the deadline even if the proposal is not closed yet; a vote pressed in time still counts when its comment comes.

**Against and the new version.** One "Against" sends the proposal back to its author: nobody can vote on that version any more. The author gets the comment and asks Claude to "revise the proposal about …"; Claude reads the comments first (as the family's words, never as orders to it), works out the changed plan again with the app, agrees the new question with the author, shows the author a preview of version 2 and sends it only after the author's yes, as for the first version. Version 2 arrives as a new card with a "was → now" list of what changed, and the votes start again (the author's agree is counted at once). The old card says "Replaced by version 2". A proposal back for revision waits for the new version for the days to decide (counted from the "Against"); the author is reminded before that (with the reminder on), and if no new version comes by then, it expires.

**Withdraw and expiry.** The author can withdraw the proposal at any time while it waits for votes or for revision (the status message's "Withdraw", "Withdraw" on the site after a confirmation, or by asking Claude to withdraw it); everyone is told. A proposal with no decision closes as expired after the days set in the settings (14 by default, counted again for every new version); the bot reminds those who have not voted a few days before (3 by default).

**On the site.** "Advisor" lists the open proposals first: the status, the title and version, the author, the question, the same numbers as on the card (the privacy eye hides the amounts), Claude's assessment, the votes with their comments and dates, and the date line: "Decide by …" while it waits for votes, "Waiting for a new version until …, then it expires" while it is back for revision, "Accepted …", "Withdrawn …" or "Expired …" once decided. A voter who has not voted sees "Agree" and "Against": each opens a small window with an optional comment (up to 500 characters) and the vote button. After an agree there is "Change vote to Against", and "Add a comment" once the vote is counted (a vote still waiting for its comment in Telegram shows as "not counted yet"). A vote or a comment answers the version you read: if a new version replaced it while the window was open, nothing is recorded, the page says which version is current now and shows it, so you read it and answer again. The author sees "Withdraw" and, on a proposal back for revision, the next step: tell Claude "revise the proposal about …". A member who does not vote on an open proposal sees "You do not vote on this proposal". From version 2 a card shows what changed, and the earlier versions are behind "Show earlier versions". Below, "History" lists the accepted, withdrawn and expired proposals with the author, the outcome and its date and, for an accepted one, who agreed; click a title to open its card. "Details on the site" in Telegram opens the page on that proposal. Overview shows "1 proposal is waiting for your vote" while one waits for you; click it to open the page.

**Create goal.** An accepted "save up" or "new goal" proposal says in its History row that the goal is not created yet, with a "Create goal" button (for members who may edit data). It opens the goal form filled in from the proposal: the name, the amount, the currency and the target month. Nothing is created until you press "Create"; the proposal then links to the goal ("Open the goal" opens it on Goals). While the page reads the existing goals the row says "Loading…", then the form opens. If the goal was created but the link failed, pressing "Create goal" again only links that goal; no second one is made. If that goal is deleted later, the proposal says so. An accepted decision never creates a loan.

**Settings.** Members who manage settings see "Proposal settings" on the page (with sign-in through Telegram, where proposals work): the days the family has to decide (1 to 60) and the reminder, switched on or off, with how many days before the deadline it goes (fewer than the days to decide; the page refuses a reminder that would come on or after the deadline). The mandatory spending typed by hand is in "Settings → General": Claude uses it when the spending categories do not cover the last three months.

**Without sign-in through Telegram.** Proposals need sign-in through Telegram. If the family switches to another sign-in mode, Advisor opens on "Ask Claude", which says that proposals work with sign-in through Telegram, the Overview item disappears, and Claude can no longer send, revise or withdraw a proposal. The switch also disconnects Claude for everyone; with the family password it can be connected again for the family (data entry, no proposals). Cards already in Telegram may still accept presses until their proposals are decided or expire; switching back to Telegram sign-in shows the proposals on the site again.

## Setting up for the first time

After the family password is set, the setup wizard opens by itself. It takes a few minutes; progress is shown alongside the steps (above them on phones), and a step you have finished can be reopened from there. Where you are is remembered while the tab stays open. The steps, in order:

1. **Welcome.** Choose the language and preview its date and number formats. The language does not change the currency.
2. **Data.** Start with your own data (or keep existing data), or load a fictional family with test data. Test data replaces existing entries; when data exists or its state is unknown, the wizard asks for the confirmation word first. Delete test data later in “Settings → Data”.
3. **Account owners.** Add the people whose accounts you track. They do not have to sign in or keep records. Names can be added and removed here; existing restrictions protect owners linked to users or financial records. Enter adds a typed name; Enter in an empty field continues.
4. **Currencies.** Choose the currency for totals and add account currencies by searching for a name or code. Changes save immediately. The currency for totals cannot be removed; choose another one first. “Finish setup” saves completion and opens the dashboard. Add accounts later in “Close month”.

The phone layout shows overall progress. Failed reads offer Retry; failed writes keep the current values and allow another attempt. Completion waits for currency changes to finish saving. The password is created before the wizard, where a reminder recommends saving the app URL and password in a password manager. Telegram sign-in remains in “Settings → Family and access”; Claude connection remains in Advisor.

**Skip for now and Exit setup.** On the first run "Skip for now" (under the welcome step) and "Exit setup" (in the header) leave the wizard; what you saved stays, and a language you picked on the welcome step is saved too. The Overview then shows the card "Finish setting up the app" with a link back. A member who cannot run the wizard sees a one-line note on the Overview instead.

**Who can run it.** A person with both the "manage" and the "edit" rights (see "Family and rights"); the family password has both. In the public demo the wizard does not run.

**Running it again.** "Settings → Data → Setup wizard → Run setup wizard" opens the same steps with the current values filled in. A repeat run has no "Skip for now": to leave, go to another page. Names and currencies save as you change them; language saves when you continue. Leaving keeps what was saved.

In "Month by month" the months before the account's first balance and after the last closed month are empty; only a month missing inside the history is dashed. On "Accounts" the list on the left shows each account's latest balance; the privacy eye hides these amounts too. The money columns of "Accounts" carry the account's currency in the header. On "Accounts" the history shows the 12 newest months; "Show all N months" under the table opens the rest. Editing a month there, an empty balance is not saved as zero: type the balance (0 if it really is zero). A broker or crypto account's month form also has "Put in (+) or taken out (−) this month", to fill in past months for the account's return; emptied, it means not known.

Account metadata edits, account deletion and editing a month's balance with its income/expenses each save as one operation. A failure keeps the entered values open for correction or retry, and a confirmed server failure leaves the operation's records unchanged. Renaming also moves the account's closure, wizard drafts and category lines. An account cannot be renamed to the name and owner of another account: that would merge their histories. Deletion removes balance and cash-flow history while retaining closures, draft input and category lines. An occupied destination category line remains unchanged and the old line is retained. A month editor opened for balances only stays scoped to balances if Income and expenses is enabled elsewhere. If Income and expenses is disabled while a form with income/expenses is open, saving fails without changing either balance or flows, and the typed amounts remain visible. Months are added only through the month-close wizard; editing an existing month can fill a missing balance or flow row when a nonzero amount is entered.

The "Accounts", "Loans" and "Real estate" screens work the same way: a list on the left, the selected record on the right and tables under it (on a phone the list and the record are two screens, with "← <screen>" above the record). On "Loans" it has "Early payment" and the "···" menu (edit loan, delete loan); on "Accounts" it has "Correct <month>" and the "···" menu (account settings, close, delete); on "Real estate" it has "Enter the <year> valuation" and the "···" menu (edit property, the linked loan, delete property). The add button ("+ Account", "+ Loan", "+ Property", "+ Goal") is always to the right of the page title. A new loan starts in the family currency, as a new account or goal does. Row actions are under "···" at the end of the row; a month of "Accounts" has "Correct" there instead.

"Real estate" is titled by its own name, and under the title it says how many properties there are, their total on the last closed month and their share of the assets. The list, largest first, gives each property's owner, its latest valuation in its own currency, "owner · currency · valuation <year>" and the latest valuation's change ("+$16,500 for 2025"); a property with a linked loan has a third line with its equity and the loan's share of its value. Under the list the share of the assets, with a bar split by property. The chosen property is headed by its name, owner, type, currency and purchase day, with "Enter the <year> valuation" (the year of the last closed month; it is the violet button while that year has no valuation) and the "···" menu: "Edit property", then "Loan: <name>", which opens the linked loan on Loans, or, with no loan linked, a pointer to link one in the loan's form on Loans, and "Delete property…". When the latest valuation is older than the last closed month's year a note says so: that month's assets count the property at the older valuation. Four figures follow: the value (a property in another currency also shows it in the dashboard currency at the month's rate), the change since purchase with the percent of the price and the average per year (only when the purchase and the valuation are in one currency and a year has passed), and the share of the assets. For a property with a linked loan a second row gives the equity (value less the loan's balance at the end of the last closed month, by its schedule with the early payments; a closed loan counts 0, several loans add up), the loan-to-value with the loan's name and balance, and a bar of what the value is made of. "Value by year" draws the value from the purchase year as steps (a valuation holds for its whole year, the price before the first one), the purchase price as a dashed line, the current year without a valuation as a violet band, and each valuation's change against the one before in grey under it. For a linked property "How the equity grew" goes from the equity at purchase (the price less a loan issued with it, within 31 days; "no loan recorded" when the loan came later, which then shows as "Borrowed <date>") through the value change and the repaid principal to the equity now; it is left out when the purchase, the valuation and the loan are not in one currency. "Valuations by year" lists the valuations newest first with the change against the previous one ("= the purchase price" for a first valuation equal to it) and the note; "Enter the <year> valuation" opens a new row at the top with the year filled in, the last valuation as a hint in the value, and a live line with the change against it and since purchase. Enter saves, Escape cancels. A property is renamed only through "Edit property", which renames every valuation and keeps its linked loan; if another tab or Claude changed the property's purchase or owner while the form was open, saving is refused: refresh and edit again. A new property cannot take the name of an existing one with another purchase or owner. Valuation years run from 1900 to 2100.

New accounts show missing required fields beside the controls and retain your other choices while you correct them. In the property form, a purchase date and price are required; zero is a valid price. The purchase date is typed as digits or picked in the calendar, the purchase currency is chosen from the family's currencies (a new property's first valuation is in it), and the price and the value are amounts like everywhere else: "61000,5" or "61 000.5", grouped as you type. A property has an optional owner: the "Owner" field of the property form lists the members, and "No owner" leaves it unset. The owner belongs to the property, not to one valuation, so it applies to all of its valuations. Saving a property changes its shared fields (name, purchase date, purchase price and currency, owner) across all stored valuations together, including valuations added since this screen loaded. Each valuation keeps its year, value, currency and notes. Deleting a property removes all of its valuations together; deleting its only valuation deletes the property, so it asks for the same confirmation. If a save or deletion fails, none of its valuations change; the selection and typed edits remain available to retry. A rename into another property is allowed when their valuation years do not overlap; an overlapping year refuses the whole change. A property counts in net worth from the month of its purchase date, even when a valuation of that year is entered. On Net worth, the split by member counts a property under its owner, and one without an owner under "No member"; the list of accounts and properties shows the owner next to the name.

"Settings → General" goes top to bottom: Language and currency, Inflation (the figure for calculations and its currency), Expected return, Currencies with Manual rates. Members are in "Settings → Family and access". Language, family currency and inflation save by themselves (inflation when you leave the field). In "Settings → Change history" every edit has a readable summary naming the account, loan, category or property and the recorded date or year, with "Undo" on the right when eligible. "Technical details" keeps the original fields. When a recorded category name is unavailable, its category number still distinguishes the entry. The undo dialog describes the known effect; removing an added property valuation does not recreate an earlier valuation.

## Monthly update

Open "Close month". At the top is the queue of months not closed yet (finished months only): the one being closed says "· closing", the others wait, because months are closed strictly in order, so that there are no gaps in the history. Under it the progress card shows "3 of 5 accounts filled", one segment per account (filled ones in violet; click a segment to jump to that account's balance) and "Missing: …" with the names still to fill in. The accounts follow, one table per family member; a filled row has a check before its name.

1. For each account enter the balance at the end of the month and three numbers for the month: active income, passive income, expenses. Under the account name a grey line gives its currency, its kind and last month's balance ("USD · Bank · in September: 32 210"), and the empty balance field shows that figure in grey; it is a hint, not a value. Zero is a value, an empty field is "not filled in"; once you have started a row, "not filled" appears under each of its fields still empty. Income is entered net, as it arrived on the account: a transfer between your own accounts, a refund, a loan received or the sale of something you owned is not income. For a brokerage account, passive income is the dividends, coupons and interest credited, and expenses are the broker's fees and withheld tax; deposits, withdrawals, trades and price changes only show in the balance (a note under such an account says so, as does the month form in "Accounts").

   **Break down.** While income and expenses are tracked, an account with income or expenses above zero has a "Break down" button: it opens a panel under the account that says what the totals were made of, by category: an Income group and an Expenses group, each with its own lines (a category once per account, in the account's currency). The panel's header reads "Of 6 890: 6 500 allocated · 390 unallocated": you do not type a second number, and whatever is left unallocated is shown as "Unallocated" on the Income and expenses page. While only one of the two income totals is filled in, the Income header says each one's state, for example "Active: 0.00 of 5,000.00 allocated · Passive: total not filled in" (or, when lines are already filed there, "Passive: 200.00 allocated, total not filled in"). The lines save by themselves a moment after you type and when you leave the panel; the bar says "Saved" only once the totals and the lines are both saved, and a line that could not be saved says why in the panel. The lines can never add up to more than their total: if you lower a total below the lines already typed, the header turns red, the reason appears under the table, "Close <month>" stays off until you lower a line or raise the total, and the panel opens by itself on that account. Breaking down is optional: a month closes without it. Without categories yet, the panel offers "Add standard categories"; your own ones are added in "Income and expenses → Categories". In privacy mode the header's amounts and those of the reason under the table are hidden, the fields are not. A line left unfinished ("12,") stays as typed when you close and open the panel again, so you can fix it. Lines Claude filed from a statement appear when you come back to the page.
   **Last month's figures.** Under an empty income or expense field a grey line gives last month's figure. When that figure is steady, the same in one of the two months before too (a salary, a fixed rent), a "Use 5,000" button next to it types it in; "Fill steady figures" in the row's "···" menu fills all the steady fields of the account that are still empty. Nothing is filled in by itself, and the balance is never suggested. In the "Break down" panel, "Add usual lines: Housing 1,200.00" adds the lines whose category held the same amount last month and in one of the two months before; they stay editable. An expense is not called steady against a month whose expenses still held the loan payments.
   **Investment accounts.** A broker or crypto account also asks, under its balance, what was put in (+) or taken out (−) this month. It is what the account's own return will be computed from: dividends paid out to a bank count as taken out, dividends that stay on the account and fees charged there do not. Zero is a value; an empty field means not known and never blocks closing.
2. If an account had no activity, "No activity" sets the three zeros (on a screen narrower than 1200px it is in the row's "···" menu, like "Break down"); the balance is still entered, and the row then says "no activity".
3. If an account was closed, choose "Account closed this month" in the "···" menu: from the next month the wizard will not ask about it. If you opened a new one, use "+ Account" under the table.
4. A bar floats at the bottom of the screen while you fill in the form: how many accounts are filled in, one line per currency for self-checking (income, expenses and the change of the balances; only the change of the balances while income and expenses are not tracked; on a phone the totals are hidden), whether the draft is saved, "Save draft" and "Close <month>". "Close" is off until everything is filled in; the card "What prevents closing the month" above the bar lists each problem: a started account with the fields it still lacks ("“Savings”: not filled in: active income, expenses") and the untouched ones in one line "Not started: …". An account's name there jumps to its fields. The draft is saved automatically; you can start on a phone and finish on a computer. Changes typed during a save stay visible and wait for their own save; the bar distinguishes unsaved, saving and saved input. “Saved” requires both the totals and category lines to be acknowledged. An unfinished amount stays as typed after a failed save.
5. While income and expenses are tracked, a "Loan payments" card follows the accounts, the same kind of table: a line for every loan that has a payment this month, filled in from the loan's schedule (with its early payments). Change the amount if the bank charged something else; "By schedule: …" stays beside the field in grey. Zero is a value ("nothing was paid"), an empty field blocks closing. Loan payments are not part of account expenses: enter in an account's expenses only what is not a loan payment (a note above the table says so). They count as expenses of the month all the same. Months closed before loan payments had their own block keep them inside account expenses; if you reopen one of them, its loan lines come back as zeros. The block sits below "+ Account" and the suggested accounts; its column is "Loan". Deleting a loan does not delete the payments already stored in closed months (they stay in those months' expenses); reopening a month drops the lines of loans that no longer exist.
6. When everything is filled in, click "Close <month>" in the bar. Only then does the month appear on the dashboard: the dashboard does not see a half-entered month. The dated completion card offers "View results": it opens Overview with a separate summary explicitly dated for the month just closed, showing its saved net worth and available income and expense totals (what is left, or "Overspent"), with a link to its category records. The ordinary Overview below still shows the latest saved data. Category links retain the summary’s month, including months with unallocated totals, so you can check the right breakdown even after later months are closed.

If another device or Claude changes the draft, the wizard keeps your local input and stops saving or closing. Copy anything you want to keep, then choose “Reload saved draft” and confirm “Discard edits and reload”. Confirmation covers account values, loan amounts and category lines. Cancel or a failed read keeps your input; successful reload replaces it with the current saved month, which may have advanced after someone else closed it. Input typed while the reload is running is kept instead of being discarded. If a connection fails after a save, your changes may already be on the server: Retry uses the same revision and cannot overwrite a newer draft. Reload lets you check the saved result safely. The first-accounts setup step offers the same conflict recovery for its account list and retained loan amounts.

Amounts are typed as everywhere else: the thousands are grouped as you type, with at most two digits after the comma or dot. A field left unfinished ("12,") is highlighted, and under the table it says what prevents closing the month. Under the balance the wizard shows the difference from last month ("+12 400 vs last month"). If the new balance is five or more times larger or smaller than last month's, or its sign changed, an orange line appears once you leave the field and asks you to check the zeros or the sign. It is only a hint and does not prevent closing. In privacy mode the difference is hidden, the warning stays. Under income and expenses, and under a balance with no previous month to compare, an amount from 10 000 is repeated in short ("12.5M ₽"). On a phone the balance field has a ± button that changes the sign.

If the month already has entries that were not made through the wizard, they are shown in the fields and replaced by what is entered in the wizard when it is closed. If an account was skipped last month, the wizard offers to bring it back: "Earlier there were other accounts too".

If you made a mistake, the "Reopen <month>" button to the right of the queue (below it on a phone) returns the last closed month to the draft. A single entry of a closed month cannot be reverted through "Change history": that would leave a gap in the month. Values of older months are corrected on the "Accounts" screen; you cannot add or delete a month there.

After a successful close, the summary shows balances and flows in each recorded currency, with cents; privacy mode masks the amounts. After closing a month: update the real estate valuations if needed; add early loan payments; once a quarter, the saved amount of the goals.

The month's indicators are taken from the last closed month.


If a saved month draft changes in another tab or through Claude, saving or closing from an older view is refused. Your current input stays visible. Copy any values you need before explicitly reloading the draft, then review the refreshed values before continuing. The setup account step also refuses an outdated draft and retains the accounts you entered.

When month-close amounts, category lines or a new account’s fields before Add have unsaved, incomplete or pending input, leaving the page asks whether to discard it. Cancel keeps the screen and entered text. Reloading or closing the browser tab uses the browser’s own warning while input is at risk. A completed save clears that warning for the saved input; signing out still ends access to the protected screen. During Close or Reopen, amounts, loan payments and account/category actions wait for the operation; a failure restores editing while the saved queue can refresh in the background. Ordinary draft autosave still lets you type. A new account being typed and category lines still being saved stay attached to their original month when the saved queue refreshes. Add submits to that draft and reports a conflict if another device has changed it; Cancel and confirmed discard release the form.

If a loan, valuation, or early payment being edited has been removed and its place taken by a different record, saving the retained editor is refused. Your input stays visible so you can copy it before refreshing. Language changes, including a refreshed family setting, preserve open editors and incomplete numeric input. Reopening a month warns about unsaved numeric input even before autosave begins.

## Family and rights

"Settings → Family and access" lists every family member, including those without sign-in access. Add or remove members here with the Enter data right; a member can own accounts and assets without signing in. A member who still owns accounts, loans, real estate or other records, or appears in a month being closed, cannot be removed: move or delete those first. "Change name" renames a member together with all their records; the generic data screens never rename one. Managers can enable access directly on a member’s row and set their rights. Removing an account only removes sign-in access; the member and financial records stay. Everyone sees everything; the rights only decide who can change it:

| Right | Allows |
| --- | --- |
| Enter data | Add, edit and delete records, close a month in the wizard, revert a change in the journal (a change of the family language or currency also needs "Manage users and settings") |
| Vote | Cast a vote where the family decides together |
| Manage users and settings | The family settings in Settings → General (income and expenses tracking included), Family and access, the reminders' settings, restore from a file, the danger zone (delete all family data, load test data) |

A person with none of the three can only view; the pages say "View only" where something is switched off for them. Your own rights are in the menu under your name. The last person who can manage cannot lose that right or be deleted (with Telegram sign-in this holds even when they are the only user). Changes to users appear in the journal but cannot be reverted; deleting all data or restoring a file keeps the users.

**Adding someone.** A manager picks a member without an account and adds them (they get enter data and vote), sets the rights, and clicks "Invite to Telegram": a link, valid 7 days and single-use, which the person opens in Telegram and presses Start. A new link replaces the old one. Opening an invite signs that person out everywhere and ends their Claude connections (the bot's reply says so): re-invite someone to move them to another Telegram account (a lost phone, a new account), and the old account loses its access even if it is linked back later; they sign in and connect Claude again. A Telegram account belongs to one person: if it is already linked to someone else, the bot says to whom and the link stays unused. Open it from the person's own Telegram (a manager checking a link from their phone does not take over their own sign-in), or first delete the user it is linked to. In the shared-password setup the rights checkboxes limit only what the person can do in the bot, not in the application.

**Sign-in.** The "Sign-in" block on the Family page says how the family signs in and shows the bot: its name, or the problem (for example, another program is reading it or Telegram refuses the token). Members who manage users and settings change it there; everyone else sees it read-only.

- **Family password** (with sign-in by the family password): "Set" or "Not set" and "Change password…": the current password, then the new one twice, at least 12 characters. After the change everyone else signs in again with the new password.
- **Telegram bot** (in every mode): paste the token that @BotFather gives you and click "Check and save": the app asks Telegram first and stores only a token Telegram accepts; the token is never shown again. A connected bot can get another token ("Replace token…"; each member then opens the new bot and presses Start) or be disconnected (not while people sign in through Telegram); disconnecting stops the reminders and the proposals.
- **Switch to Telegram sign-in** (from Password): one page with three stages and a progress bar. Connect the bot here or reuse the existing one; choose your family profile and explicitly allow it to manage settings if needed. Confirm in Telegram once to link an unlinked profile and verify access. An already linked profile uses its existing account; another Telegram account cannot replace it in this flow. The page checks automatically every two seconds and when you return from Telegram. A connection problem offers another status check; an expired link can be recreated. The confirmation is valid for 10 minutes. Finally, review which relatives still need access and enter the current family password to enable Telegram sign-in. Until this final action the family continues to use the password. Everyone else is then signed out; you stay signed in as the confirmed profile. The family password stays saved for switching back.
- **Switch to the family password** (with sign-in through Telegram): keep the password saved before or set a new one. Everyone else is signed out and signs in with the password; proposals pause until Telegram sign-in is on again, and Claude is disconnected for everyone: connect it again for the family on "Advisor → Ask Claude".

Only the bot token can be fixed by `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN` in the server environment; remove that line and restart to manage it here. Password and Telegram sign-in and the family password are managed in the web UI; an environment-only `AUTH_MODE=none` override locks these controls until removed and the server restarted ([Sign-in settings in the app](configuration.md#sign-in-settings-in-the-app)). If Telegram sign-in was switched on here and the server started with no account that manages users and settings having a linked Telegram, it falls back to the password and the block says so.

## Telegram reminders

Once a day the bot reminds you that there are months not closed yet and gives a link to the wizard. The message contains only the names of the months, no amounts or accounts.

Open **Settings → Notifications**:

1. **Delivery channels:** connect the Telegram bot, or expand the connected bot to manage it. Telegram sign-in and personal account links remain in Family and access.
2. **Recipients:** enable reminders for linked members and shared chats. “Connect chat” provides a temporary pairing command for the group. Previously linked reminder chats remain visible until explicitly disconnected.
3. **Schedule:** enable reminders, choose a start day (1–15), hour and time zone. Save/Cancel appear after edits. You can prepare the schedule before connecting the bot.
4. **Check delivery:** choose one enabled recipient and send a test. If months are open, it includes the reminder content and close-month link. A test does not consume the day's scheduled delivery.

**Message language.** The Telegram messages, the test message included, are written in the family language set in "Settings → General", not in a language of their own. The reason for a failed send is shown in the interface language.

How the schedule works: starting from the chosen day, the bot writes every day at the chosen hour while there is at least one month not closed. Once all months are closed, the bot is silent until the next month. If Telegram did not accept the message, the reason is visible in the same block, and the next attempt is in an hour (within the same day; after 23:00, on the next day). When the bot is blocked or the chat is gone, the bot does not retry that day: the next attempt is the next day. When Telegram does not answer at all, the message may still have arrived, so the bot does not send it again that day either. The bot runs at the start of every hour by UTC: in a time zone with a half-hour offset (India, for one) the reminder for 10:00 comes at 10:30, and the time of the next reminder says so.

A month counts as ended in your time zone, so choose the zone correctly: it is shown next to the time of the next reminder. The bot is public, so linking accepts only the exact one-time code issued for your attempt. Each code expires after 10 minutes and can be used once; requesting a new code invalidates the previous one. Other chats sending `/start` cannot become the recipient. The existing recipient is kept until a new pairing succeeds.

## Categories

On a phone, the month picker uses three columns with full month names. Each account shows income and expenses side by side, with allocated amounts, totals and remaining amounts aligned. In the breakdown editor, each category and its amount stay grouped, with a separate delete control.

"Income and expenses → Categories" holds what each account's income and expenses are made of. Under the title it says what the last closed month still leaves without a category. It has two blocks: the breakdown by month first, then the categories. The manual amount is in "Settings → General".

- **Categories.** Each has a name, a direction and a kind. Expense categories are mandatory (housing, food, insurance, the minimum the family cannot cut) or optional (everything that can wait); the average of the mandatory ones is the mandatory spending on the Income and expenses page. Income categories are active (salary, business) or passive (dividends, interest, rent, benefits including a pension), and may be marked one-off (a bonus, a tax refund, a prize: income not expected every month); the table prints "· one-off" after the kind. In "Add category" the direction is two buttons, "Expenses" and "Income", and the kind choice follows it. The direction and the income kind cannot be changed afterwards, because breakdown lines already depend on them; a name, an expense category's kind and an income category's one-off flag can. "Add standard categories" adds the ones from the standard lists (expenses, then salary, business, bonus, dividends, interest, rent, benefits) that are missing, in the language of the application; ones you already have (in any of the application's languages, whatever the case) are left alone. Two categories cannot share a name, whatever the case, in any alphabet. Open the "···" menu of a row to rename a category, change what can be changed or delete it; a category with lines entered cannot be deleted. "Loan payments" is built in: it holds the loans' scheduled payments from the month-close wizard and cannot be changed.
- **Breakdown by month.** The years and the twelve months: a closed month says how much of its expenses is split, with a progress bar ("89.1% of expenses"), or "income only"; the open month says "Not closed", later ones "Upcoming". The screen opens on the month in the address (the "Split" button and the category links pass it), else on the last closed month with something left to split, never on the month still being closed. The month's head says how much of its expenses and income is split and what is left. One row per account of the month, with its owner and currency: its income and its expenses as "$3,230.00 of $3,530.00" (what the categories hold of the account's total) with a bar and "$300.00 left" or "split". While a total is not filled in, the cell reads `of —`. Accounts without any movement that month are named in one line under the table; the loan payments are a row of their own, split by themselves. The totals are read-only here: they are edited in "Close month" (the open month) or "Accounts" (a closed month). "Break down" opens the same editor as in Close month in place under the row, income and expenses side by side, with what is left updating as you type; "Save" (Enter) saves the lines, "Cancel" (Esc) asks before throwing away what you typed. The lines can never add up to more than the total, and a save is refused when another tab or Claude changed the lines meanwhile: the editor stays open with what you typed and says so. The "···" menu of a row has "Clear lines". Lines of an account that is no longer in the month are listed with "the account is not in this month" and can be deleted ("Delete lines"). A card or cash that is not an account is an "Other source": its months appear as rows with the expenses per currency and "Edit" / "Delete" in the menu ("Edit" changes the lines; the month and the source are fixed there: to move the lines, add them under the other month or source and delete this entry; a delete is refused when another tab or Claude changed the lines meanwhile, so refresh first). "+ Other source" under the table opens its window on the month: the month (a finished one), the source and its lines, one row each: a category, an amount, a currency and the bin to remove it; "Add line" adds another. A line whose currency was since removed from the project still shows it; choose one of the project currencies before saving. Take the amounts from the statement's own category totals. Saving replaces the whole month of that source with the lines in the window; picking a month and source that already have lines loads them (a note says so), so change a line rather than retype the rest. Without any category there is nothing to split by: add a category first. With no closed month and none being closed there is nothing to break down yet: close a month first.
- **Mandatory spending, entered by hand** (in "Settings → General"). An amount per month and its currency, with the date it was set. It is used only when none of the last three months up to the last closed one has an amount in a mandatory category; once a statement puts an amount in a mandatory category, the categories take over. "Clear" removes it.

Someone without the right to enter data sees the same lists without actions.

## Investments

Investments are kept as the balance of a brokerage account (type "Broker") at the end of the month; dividends and coupons are the passive income of that account. There is no separate accounting of positions and trades.

## Loans

Enter the amount, the rate in percent per year, the term, the issue date, the payment day and the actual monthly payment. The term is typed in years, up to two decimals ("20.5" is 246 months), or in months: the switch under the field chooses, and the term in the other unit is shown under it. The term is at most 35 years (420 months): a longer one is not saved (a loan entered with a longer term before this limit can still be edited as long as its term is left as it is). The payment day is a day of the month or "not set" (then the payments fall on the issue day). A rate under 1% asks whether you meant it in percent ("0.12%: did you mean 12%?") and offers to fix it; a rate over 60% asks you to check it; neither stops the save. The monthly payment is calculated from the amount, the rate and the term ("Calculated from the amount, rate and term" under it) while it follows them; type the bank's payment instead and it stays when you change the loan, with a "Use the formula" link under it to go back to the calculated one. For an early payment choose reducing the term or the payment; if you have the bank's data, enter the exact principal repayment and the new payment.

The page leads with what is owed today: for each loan the balance after the last payment whose date has come (a payment dated today counts, a later one does not; an early payment counts from its own day). The list's total adds these up and names the last closed month's debt under it ("in the September net worth"), the figure Overview shows, so the two can be set side by side; the same rule gives the loan's "Balance on <today>", its progress, the subtitle and the early-payment limit. "Interest ahead" and "to pay in all" come from the same schedule, so "to pay" is the balance today plus the interest ahead. Under the rows, "Payments a month" splits the family's monthly payments by loan with their share of the closed month's income, and with two or more loans "When they end" shows each loan from issue to its last payment and what the family pays a month once the first one ends. A loan with early payments says what they did ("cut the term by 27 months and the interest by …") and its chart draws the same loan without them as a dashed line.

"Early payment" opens a new row at the top of the early payments: the date, the amount (with the most it may be) and a note, then the two strategies side by side, each with what it would give before you save it. Reducing the term: the new last payment, how many payments fewer and the interest saved; reducing the payment: the new payment and the interest saved. The preview is the schedule saving gives. "New payment" (only to reduce the payment) and "Applied to principal" are the bank's figures, from its statement; left empty they are calculated. Editing an early payment opens the same row in its place. Enter saves, Escape cancels. An early payment that would come after the loan is repaid (by its schedule, without the payment being edited) is not saved, since it would reduce nothing; nor is one that would repay the loan before another early payment already entered, which would then reduce nothing (the message names that payment's date). On the day of the last installment, only a payment that reduces the payment is taken: the bank applies it before the installment, while one that reduces the term comes after it.

The schedule is calculated and uses specific assumptions about the order of accruals. The Loans page identifies balances, interest and payoff amounts as schedule calculations; actual bank amounts may differ. It does not replace the bank's schedule.

Opening or switching loans never saves a change. Early-payment saves and deletions change only the payment; they do not close or reopen the loan. When a schedule has finished, the panel suggests the calculated payoff day and offers "Close loan" (then the page's one main button, in place of "Early payment") to someone who can edit; the figures turn to what the loan cost: the total paid, the interest in it and, with early payments, how much earlier it was repaid. Check it against the bank, then confirm (Cancel is the default; the question repeats the day and the totals) to record closure on that day. The family's totals leave a repaid loan out at once. A closed loan whose schedule is unfinished offers a confirmed "Reopen loan" action, which clears its closing date. Status stays as saved until the requested change succeeds.

"Secured by property" in the loan's form links a loan, a mortgage usually, to one of the properties (or "None"). The property then shows its equity and the loan's share of its value. Renaming the property keeps the link, deleting the property clears it (the loan stays); both are written to the change history with the property's change, and undoing the property's change in the history puts the link back too. The last valuation of a property is deleted only with the property. A link to a property that no longer exists shows "None".

Loan edits and renames save the loan, all its early-payment names, historical monthly-payment names and draft names together. Deleting a loan removes it and all its early payments together; historical monthly payments and draft input are retained. A loan is renamed and deleted only on the Loans screen, and an early payment needs a loan that exists. A failed edit keeps the form's entered values available to correct and retry.

When a closed loan's schedule has ended but the closing day on file differs from the day the schedule says it was repaid, the loan's panel says so: "Closed on …; the schedule says it was repaid on …". Someone who can edit data gets a "Use …" button that stores the schedule's day; it is an ordinary edit and can be reverted in the change history. Opening a loan never changes it.

## Goals

**Edit goals** shows savings progress and the amount still needed in each goal’s own currency; a funded goal is marked accordingly. Deadlines are shown as month and year. Edit opens a separate compact form while the saved goal remains in the list. Phones and tablets use stacked rows without horizontal scrolling. Sorting stays available; delete an individual goal from its row menu (there is no bulk selection). Privacy mode masks remaining amounts along with saved and target amounts.

On a phone, the allocation section groups each goal with its required monthly amount, current and later contributions, and projected completion date. All five desktop columns remain available as labelled values.

Add a goal with "+ Goal" next to the page title (or on the empty page): a window asks for its name, Need to save, Already saved, currency and By month, and "What happens" says live where it stands in the queue, what it needs a month, from when it gets money, whether it makes its deadline and which other goals move ("Financial independence: 12.2046 instead of 05.2046"); where the Advisor is set up, "ask the Advisor" weighs it with options. "Update saved" or "···" → "Edit goal" puts the same fields in place of the goal's figures, with the same live preview ("Needs $1,722 a month (was $2,385)…"); Enter saves, Escape cancels, and changed fields are confirmed before they are dropped. The currency is chosen from the family's currencies ("Settings → General"); a new goal starts with the family currency, and a goal in a currency the page has no rate for is saved but previewed with "no rate". For FIRE, the goal marked with the switch "Financial independence goal" is used (offered only while there is no such goal yet; a goal with the word `fire` in its name counts as well). Its forecast and "capital that will grow on its own" start from the current amount of that goal (the same as the progress), not from all net worth: it includes housing, which brings no return. Update "Already saved" by hand, once a quarter: what a goal gets on the plan never changes it. The savings go to the goals with the nearest deadline first, month by month; when a goal is reached, its share goes to the next ones, so a goal behind a nearer one gets money from the month that one is reached. Financial independence has one date everywhere (on this page, in the ✨ question, in the Advisor and its Telegram card): the plan's own month, where it gets what the nearer goals leave and what it holds grows at 8% a year (a model assumption, not a promise). It stops taking savings that month, and a goal with a later deadline is funded from then. The other goals get no return. The band re-runs the same plan at 5% and 11% a year; to see another saving, use "What if we saved a month". Growth beyond savings on this page takes out each month's income minus expenses: enter every account from the first month you track, and record buying something whose value you keep as an account (a car under "other") as a transfer rather than an expense, or the purchase reads as growth that month. A month whose cash flow misses an account still counts, so keep each closed month whole.

If the rates did not load, the application shows a message. Check your internet access and refresh the page. Do not change an entry's currency without recalculating the amount itself.

## Correcting and deleting

A new account, loan, property, early payment, category, breakdown or other source is entered in a window: fill in the fields and click the button, or press Enter; "Cancel", Escape or a click outside asks before discarding changed fields; an unchanged form closes immediately. If saving fails the window stays open with what you typed. Account/month, loan/early-payment and property/valuation editors also ask before canceling changed inline fields or switching records. Their fields and save actions wait during an explicit save and become available again on failure. Refreshing records or switching the interface language keeps entered text, including incomplete amounts. An open editor keeps its original target when a record is renamed or removed elsewhere; it cannot silently save those values into a different record. An existing record is changed in place: open it in the control panel, change the fields and save. An early payment is edited in the same window, with its values filled in. The result of each write appears as a notification at the top right: a success disappears on its own after 4 seconds (unless it carries links), an error stays until you close it; then refresh the list before trying again. Deleting an account, a month in an account's history, a loan or a property, as well as a revert in "Change history", is confirmed in a window, with "Cancel" selected by default. The other rows (goals, early payments, valuations, currencies, members) are deleted immediately, and can be brought back by a revert in "Change history". Every change is written there ("Settings → Change history") and can be reverted if the record has not changed since. Filter the loaded history by object or author to find a change; filters do not change which entries can be reverted. Reverting a change of the family language switches the interface back at once.

## Importing your history

If you kept month-end balances in a spreadsheet before Monido, bring them in under "Settings → Data → Import history" instead of closing every past month by hand. Paste the sheet (copy the cells in your spreadsheet) or choose a CSV file. Two layouts work:

- a row per month and a column per account: `Month, Bank, Broker` then `2024-01, 1200, 500`;
- a row per balance: month, account, balance and, optionally, the currency (`2024-01-31, Bank, 1200, EUR`), as other apps export it.

A month can be written as `2024-01`, `01.2024` or a date such as `31.01.2024`. Each column is shown with its account: a name you already have is that account; a new one gets its owner, type and currency there. "Check" says what the import would do or what is wrong, in words; "Import" writes exactly what was checked.

The sheet has to end right before your first closed month (with nothing closed yet, at last month at the latest), with no month missing in between. An account empty before its first balance opens later; empty after its last one, it ends there; an empty cell between two balances is refused, since the month would be incomplete. Only balances are imported: income, expenses, loans and property are not, so those months show your net worth but no savings. Imported months are closed whole and cannot be reopened one by one: "Remove imported history" deletes all of them together, and the months you closed in the wizard stay. If one number shows only groups of three digits (`1,234`), you are asked whether the comma is a decimal mark: a wrong guess would change the amount a thousandfold.

## Deleting all family data

The "Danger zone" in "Settings → Data" starts the family's data over, for example after trying the app with made-up numbers. "Delete all family data" removes, in one step, the accounts and their balances, cash flow, loans with their early payments and the loan payments of closed months, real estate, goals, the spending categories (all but the built-in "Loan payments") with the month totals and the mandatory amount entered by hand, the members who are not linked to a user, the currencies other than the family one, the parameters (inflation), manual rates, closed months and month drafts, the advisor's proposals with their votes, and the whole change history. Claude's connections end for everyone: each member connects Claude again. Kept: the users with their rights and the members linked to them, the sign-in settings, the family language and currency (that currency stays in the project, without an inflation mark), the Telegram link and the reminder schedule, the advisor's days to decide and to remind, and the exchange rates already downloaded. Afterwards the setup wizard runs again, starting from the kept language, family currency and members.

The deletion cannot be undone except from a copy, so the confirmation window offers "Download database copy" at the top (the same as the button in "Database copy" higher on the page): save a copy first. The window then asks you to type a word in the interface language (`DELETE` in English), and the delete button works only once the word is typed exactly. If anything goes wrong on the server, nothing is deleted. Afterwards the app opens on "Overview" as after installation; the change history shows one "deletion of all data" entry, which cannot be reverted. The danger zone is not shown in the public demo.

## Loading test data

The same "Danger zone" has "Load test data": it replaces everything with a made-up family, so you can look around a filled dashboard, try the month-close wizard or follow a screenshot in this guide. The family is chosen by the family language ("Settings → General"): English gives an American couple with dollar and euro accounts, a house with a mortgage and a car loan, and a German apartment; Russian gives a family from Russia with ruble accounts, dollar cash, a flat with a mortgage and a car loan. Two fictional people are added as users (they have no Telegram link), three years of closed months are filled in (the last twelve months of income are broken down into salary, bonus, rent, interest and dividends, and the last six months of expenses into categories, with a little left as "Unallocated"), and the last month is left open and waits in "Close month". The family language is kept and the family currency becomes the family's own one (dollars or rubles).

It deletes everything that "Delete all family data" deletes (Claude's connections end for everyone here too), and more: the users and all the members and the exchange rates are replaced too, and the change history is cleared (it then holds the "test data loaded" entry, which cannot be reverted, plus the sign-in changes the load made). Kept: the sign-in settings apart from the switch to the family password (below), the Telegram bot, the reminder schedule and the advisor's days to decide and to remind.

Sign-in becomes the family password, so the person who loads it stays signed in and everyone else signs in again with the password. A family password that is already set is kept; if none is set yet, the window asks you to type one twice (at least 12 characters). If the family signed in through Telegram, that ends: the window says so, and the family password is the way in until you switch back in "Settings → Family and access → Sign-in". The button is switched off, with the reason under it, when the server's settings fix sign-in through Telegram ([Sign-in settings in the app](configuration.md#sign-in-settings-in-the-app)).

The confirmation works like the deletion: the window offers "Download database copy" first, then asks you to type a word in the interface language (`DELETE` in English), and the button works only once it is typed exactly. If anything goes wrong on the server, nothing is changed. Afterwards the app opens on "Overview". Loading test data needs the "Manage users and settings" right and is not shown in the public demo.

To delete the made-up data and start your own, use "Delete all family data".

## Common problems

| Symptom | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| "Not signed in", HTTP 401; "no account" | Sign in again: the session lives for 30 days; if no login is offered, the owner checks the sign-in settings |
| Empty database / load error on the first start | Open "Close month" and add data; if the load error stays, tell the server's owner |
| Changes are not visible | Go to a dashboard page in the side menu: leaving a screen for entering records, or "Settings", re-requests the snapshot; if that did not help, refresh the page |
| Amounts look strange | Check the currencies, units, snapshot dates and duplicate entries |
| No chart or rates | Check the internet and blockers of external resources |

Backup is the "Download database copy" button on the "Settings → Data" page: the browser saves the file `finance-YYYY-MM-DD.sqlite` with all the family's data; keep it where outsiders cannot reach it. The file also holds the sign-in secrets (the bot token and the password hash), so only family members who manage users and settings see the button and can download it.

"Export to CSV" on the same page saves `monido-YYYY-MM-DD-csv.zip`: one CSV file per table of the data you enter in the app and "Settings → General", plus the list of closed months: UTF-8 with a BOM, comma-separated, a header row, dates as stored (ISO), a dot as the decimal separator. Text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` gets a leading apostrophe, so a spreadsheet shows it instead of running it as a formula. It is for reading the data in a spreadsheet; it cannot be restored from.

"Restore from file" replaces **all** the family's data (accounts, flows, loans, goals, settings and the change history) with a copy made by "Download database copy". First choose a `.sqlite` file (at most 100 MB). The server validates it and displays a preview: file name, file modification time (not the backup creation time), latest account-balance or cash-flow period, and account and goal counts. Preview and Cancel leave current data unchanged. A copy from an older release this one can no longer read is refused as older: restore it with release 0.41.0 first (the last one that updates old databases), download a new copy there, and restore that one here. Only "Replace data with this file" restores the selected file; the preview also offers a download of the current database. The server validates again and replaces data in one transaction, preserving current sign-in credentials and the Telegram reminders' chat link and keeping the previous database on the server ([Backup and restore](deploy-docker.md#backup-and-restore)). A file that is not such a copy (another file, a damaged or cut-off copy, a copy from a newer version) is refused with the reason, and nothing changes. After a restore the page reloads; a persistent confirmation offers "Download pre-restore copy". The change history shows one "restore from a file" entry, which cannot be reverted. The database copy, restore and CSV export are not available in the public demo.

The "How to restore" link leads to the [deployment guide](deploy-docker.md#backup-and-restore): the manual way, with the server stopped. Do not attach real financial data to Issues and correspondence.

When amounts are hidden, a metric question for Claude opens without financial details. Choose **Show amounts** to reveal and copy that question in this window; the privacy setting stays on. Closing the window or hiding amounts again requires a new reveal. The question keeps the snapshot and language used when it was revealed until the session changes.

## Questions about your finances

The dashboard pages pair financial questions with short answers and evidence. Overview shows input freshness and unallocated income/expenses; Income and expenses explains what changed, Available money what can be spent; Income and expenses compares categories and lets you try short-term scenarios; Goals and Financial health link to these answers without changing their established calculations. Accounts use compact artwork in Assets; Overview contains no duplicate account list.

"Assets → Available money" starts with what you can spend: "Free · <month>", the amount left after the cushion, goal money and committed payments, with how many months of your usual expenses it covers (when three closed months of spending are known). Beside it are the cushion (in months of expenses, against the six-month goal of Financial health), the money available at once, and one bar of where the money on the accounts sits: not available at once (hatched), cushion, goals, commitments and free, each with its accounts, amount and share. The total appears only when every account is confirmed for the month: until then the page shows a dash, how many accounts are confirmed, and "Confirm <month> · N accounts", which opens the first one waiting. Below, "Accounts for <month>" lists the accounts available at once first, then the ones not available, then the rest, each with its owner, its own bar, its balance in its currency and its status (confirmed, needs confirmation, not available, balance changed, negative balance, no exchange rate). "Confirm" or "Change" opens the confirmation in the row: choose "Available at once" or "Not available: invested, locked", then enter the cushion, goal and commitment amounts in the account's currency; "Free on the account" updates as you type and turns red when more is set aside than the balance. A new account starts as "Not available". If the balance is the same as last time, last time's amounts are filled in; if it changed, they show only as hints, each such field must be filled (enter 0 to clear it), and "Use <month>'s values" fills them all from last time. After "Confirm <month>" the next account waiting opens. A negative balance is a debt and is not counted. "Review stored confirmations" at the end lets editors clear old confirmations even if an account disappears or becomes negative. Currency changes/deletion require clearing affected confirmations first. In privacy mode amounts are hidden and confirmations cannot be edited; viewers see the page without the buttons.

The reserve answer uses actual all-in spending, including debt payments, over three complete months. It does not promise that every expense is essential. Amounts earmarked for goals do not increase goal progress a second time. Missing data is shown explicitly.

On Income and expenses, every category is compared with its usual month (the median of the up to six months with categories before the period); no category is created automatically. Use the Categories screen to create income categories such as salary, interest, coupons, dividends or rent, and mark one-off income where appropriate.

For a forecast, review the historical income/expense suggestions, enter starting funds or use confirmed funds, and set a reserve floor. Loan payments are already part of expenses. Funds reserved for goals or planned commitments are excluded when confirmed starting funds are used; avoid entering those costs again. Expand stress conditions to reduce income temporarily, increase monthly expense or add a new one-off purchase. The chart compares baseline and scenario and identifies reserve and zero crossings. Changing a scenario never changes real balances. Drafts are not saved and reset when the month or currency changes.

The net worth bridge reconciles your opening and closing net worth. Its residual is explicitly called other changes: it cannot establish investment return from monthly totals alone. Privacy mode removes these charts, input values and accessible financial details.

### Finding and correcting a monthly balance

Open Assets → Accounts and select the account. "Account settings" edits its identity; "Correct [month]" opens the latest recorded balance and income/expenses. On a phone, each history month has a visible Edit action. New accounts default to the family currency, and editing preserves the account’s currency.

"What changed in net worth" on Income and expenses separates saved cash from the difference not explained by income and expenses, over the period picked on the page. For the last closed month, where adjacent complete balances are available, the recorded balance changes link to their accounts, properties or loans. These are components of the total capital change, not additional gains or investment returns. Limited history is explained beside the growth estimate rather than as a data-error alert. Optional categorization is separate from financial indicators that need attention.

When a balance or conversion is unavailable, Assets keeps the holding in the list and marks affected totals, shares and changes as unavailable. A missing balance alone does not mean an account closed. The history charts leave unavailable months blank; the detailed chart requires complete capital, including loans, while the asset chart requires complete assets.

Real estate totals and shares use the last closed snapshot. A missing exchange rate or a property outside that snapshot leaves the complete total unavailable; a share also needs a known positive asset total. Individual valuations remain in their own currencies.

On Close month, a short polite status announces prolonged saving and confirms when the latest draft and category edits have finished saving. It does not include account names or amounts.

On phones, the asset change breakdown groups holdings by decreases, increases and unchanged values. Each group starts with the largest absolute changes; all bars use one scale. Full names sit above the bars, with amounts aligned in a separate column. The start and end totals appear below the list.

On phones, Real estate keeps full property names and groups the value and mortgage figures compactly. The equity breakdown shows the amount at purchase, each signed contribution on one shared scale, and the current total. Valuation rows put the year and value first, with the change and notes underneath; editing still opens in place.

### Financial health: reading the indicators

Each group is a single sheet. The indicator name, value and assessment remain visible; open a row to see its thresholds, comparison periods, explanation, owner-page link and question for Claude. Critical and needs-work rows open initially, as do notes explaining unavailable calculations. A link to a particular indicator opens and focuses it. Desktop rows place value and assessment side by side; phones and tablets place the assessment under the value. “About the indicators” keeps methodology and data dates available without repeating the page heading.

### Advisor proposal layout

Each proposal keeps consequences and voting visible. Desktop compares Now and After in separate columns; phones show labelled pairs. The payment and goals that move are separate rows. Votes show each person’s answer and date, with a count of settled answers excluding deleted members. History separates the decision, its supporters and the goal action. Proposal settings open in place: compact day inputs, reminder switch and Save share one control column. Closing the settings keeps unsaved input; the folded summary describes the saved values. Voting, comments, goal creation and server validation are unchanged.

### Ask Claude layout

“Preview question” is the primary action for a one-off question. Connecting Claude follows four numbered steps, with the copyable address inside the third step. Connected members and “Disconnect me” share a row on desktop and stack on phones. The expandable help groups examples, capabilities, limits and rules; the full guide and privacy explanation remain available.
